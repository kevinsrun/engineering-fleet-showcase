# From hackathon execution to a reusable engineering system

I'm a Brown University PLME student working across biotechnology research, medical AI startups, hackathons, computational research, infrastructure, and public health technology. I began Fleet because the number of projects I wanted to advance exceeded my capacity to implement them sequentially, and hiring a software engineering team was out of reach.

Hackathons made the problem concrete: ambitious projects require specialized systems to come together quickly, within tight resource and time constraints. Integration and verification matter as much as producing code. Fleet is my attempt to carry that engineering discipline across an entire portfolio.

## BactroGen · Hack-Nation MIT

**Second Place, Databricks Track.** BactroGen is a nine-agent computational bacteriocin discovery platform spanning computational research, mechanistic simulation, biological evidence retrieval, and experimental planning.

The connection to Fleet is a shared interest in coordinating specialized computational work. BactroGen's nine-agent design is a description of that project; it is not evidence of nine Fleet workers or a Fleet-managed hackathon run.

## Concord · HackHarvard

**Regeneron Healthcare Track Honorable Mention.** Concord addresses clinical trial site feasibility, financial coordination, and operational optimization, with continued technical development and verification after the event.

Fleet's retained audit examined the located Concord financial-work logs and found zero Fleet provider dispatches there. It did not establish that Fleet implemented those hackathon tasks. The absence is specific to the inspected records, not a claim that every historical activity was observable.

## What I learned, and what I built next

My personal account is that these experiences shaped how I organize technically ambitious work: divide objectives, preserve context, make dependencies explicit, and require evidence before calling work complete. Fleet aims to make that workflow repeatable across projects, with an operator setting strategy and engineering units returning independently assessable results.

The source concretely implements task and evidence workflows, routing mechanisms, retained artifacts, verification, and an operator dashboard. Historical bounded coding demonstrations support a narrower claim about those workflows. Measured improvement in overall portfolio productivity or human attention remains a future evaluation.
